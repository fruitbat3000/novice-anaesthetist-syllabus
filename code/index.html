Guidance for novice anaesthetists

The first milestone of anaesthetics training is the Initial Assessment of Competence (IAC), usually achieved within three to six months (full-time equivalent). This site sets out the knowledge and skills required, a suggested order of study, and the relevant e-Learning Anaesthesia (e-LA) sessions for each topic.

Two core principles

Anaesthesia aims to facilitate a procedure the patient could not otherwise tolerate, while maintaining acceptable physiology (acceptable, not necessarily normal).

DO2 = CO × Hb × SpO2 × 1.39

The simplified oxygen delivery equation underpins much of perioperative management.

Early clinical practice

  • Where possible, request lists with a rapid turnover of fit patients and spontaneously breathing cases.
  • In the first weeks, the supervisor may complete the anaesthetic record, allowing the trainee to concentrate on the patient, the monitoring and the surgical field.
  • Develop bag-mask ventilation with either hand. Maintaining a facemask airway for whole cases builds proficiency.
  • Flush the cannula immediately after every intravenous drug.
  • For each case, consider the alternative anaesthetic techniques available and discuss with the supervisor why the chosen technique was preferred.
  • Record learning contemporaneously on the RCoA Lifelong Learning Platform (LLP), linked to the IAC Key Capabilities.

    The Initial Assessment of Competence

    The IAC is the first milestone of the 2021 RCoA anaesthetics curriculum. It is awarded when your training faculty trusts you to do two Entrustable Professional Activities (EPAs) at supervision level 2B.

    EPA 1

    Perform an anaesthetic pre-operative assessment, recognise what increases risk and tell a senior colleague: severe co-morbidity (ASA III or above), previous anaesthetic problems, and a known or predicted difficult airway.

    EPA 2

    Provide general anaesthesia for ASA I/II patients having uncomplicated surgery. In practice this prepares you to anaesthetise low-risk patients for unplanned or emergency surgery on call.

    Supervision level 2B

    Level 2B means your supervisor is in the hospital, available for queries and able to give prompt help. The IAC does not mean you are ready for independent practice. You still work under senior oversight.

    1. 1 Supervisor in theatre throughout
    2. 2A Supervisor in the theatre suite, checking in regularly
    3. 2B Supervisor in the hospital, available promptly IAC
    4. 3 Supervisor on call from home
    5. 4 Independent

    Key Capability clusters

    Link evidence on the LLP to each cluster. You do not need an assessment for every capability. The topics below are the syllabus topics that support each cluster.

    Evidence you collect

    • Supervised Learning Events (A-CEX, DOPS, CBD, ALMAT). Low-stakes feedback, with supervision levels to show progress over time. There is no minimum number.
    • Personal activities: courses, departmental teaching, e-learning and simulation.
    • Reflections, with no patient-identifiable information.
    • Logbook: the range of cases, especially airway management.

    Simulation you must do

    • Discuss and rehearse the QRH "unknowns" (usually in months 0 to 3).
    • Rehearse, then be assessed on, the failed intubation routine on a manikin, following the DAS guidelines, before sign-off.

    How sign-off works

    1. You record learning on the LLP and link it to the Key Capability clusters.
    2. Your Educational Supervisor reviews your progress and portfolio.
    3. Your ES starts a Multiple Trainer Report, completed by at least three consultants.
    4. You start the HALOs for EPAs 1 and 2, link the MTR, and send them to your ES.
    5. You start the IAC certificate, add your simulation, link the MTR, and choose your ES and College Tutor as signatories.

    The decision is a holistic judgement by the training faculty, drawing on your LLP, the MTR, your logbook, simulation and what they have seen you do.

    Source: RCoA IAC Workbook, EPAs 1 & 2 (v1.2, 2022) and the RCoA Novice Guide. The workbook is the authoritative source and should be read in full.
